01 Research

Noise, structure
& the purpose of information.

My work brings together quantum Shannon theory, entanglement theory, operator algebras, and high-dimensional geometry.

I / INFORMATION TASKS

Communication beyond transmission

In identification, the receiver only needs to test whether the transmitted message matches a specified candidate, as opposed to reconstructing it fully. I use geometric and probabilistic methods to bound the capabilities of quantum channels for this task. Recent work characterizes identification capacity for sufficiently low-noise channels and establishes its strict superadditivity.

II / HYBRID MEMORIES

What can a quantum channel preserve?

Finite-dimensional algebras describe memories combining classical and quantum information. With Robert Salzmann, I study their exact transmission through noisy channels. A channel can have several incompatible optimal memories, making the intended use essential to choosing what to encode.

III / QUANTUM CHANNELS

The limits of noisy communication

How can we tell whether a channel can reliably transmit qubits? My work develops perturbative tests for positive quantum capacity and studies the information that survives long periods of Markovian noise. Related work concerns discriminating such noise in the long-time or long-distance limit.

IV / ENTANGLEMENT & SYMMETRY

Symmetries as a guide to quantum theory

I investigate how diagonal symmetries affect the structure of quantum states and channels. With Ion Nechita, I proved the PPT² conjecture for all diagonally covariant maps, constraining entanglement distribution through this broad class of symmetric channels.

Looking ahead

My broader goal is to develop a theory of task-dependent protection. How can encoding and decoding be tailored specifically to a given task, and what physical resources are required to implement this coding procedure?
